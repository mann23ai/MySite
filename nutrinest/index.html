What it does

Four things, done properly

Not a diet app pointed at a child. A planning tool for the person doing the feeding.

Targets that fit the child

Daily values drawn from the National Academies Dietary Reference Intakes, split by age band and sex — so a four-year-old and a nine-year-old aren't measured against the same numbers.

Allergen safety, not a warning label

Set each child's allergies once. Search, planning, lunchboxes and the grocery list all flag matching foods, and logging one takes a deliberate confirmation.

A week planned in one tap

Fill the week from your own ingredients, your family's history, your favorites, or something new — aimed at whatever nutrients have been running low.

Kid Choice

Hand your child a picker filled only with foods you've already approved and that are safe for them. They feel in charge; you keep control.

Today

Know how today is going, per child

One screen per child showing live progress against their own goals. Portions scale to age automatically — a five-year-old's suggested serving is not an adult's — and you can override any value for pediatrician-directed targets.

Allergen safety

The check happens before the mistake

Foods are tagged against the nine major allergens. A match against the child's profile is flagged wherever it appears — in search, in a generated plan, in the lunchbox, in the grocery list — and logging it requires explicit confirmation.

We're precise about the limit: NutriNest covers ingredients listed on the label, not cross-contact. Always check the packaging yourself.

Trends

See what's missing — and what fills it

Day, week and month views per nutrient against the goal line. When something runs consistently short, NutriNest says so plainly and suggests kid-friendly, allergy-safe foods dense in whatever is lacking.

There's also a pediatrician summary: a clean 7- or 30-day average you can screenshot or share from the appointment chair.

Gap detection

70%

If a nutrient averages below 70% of a child's goal across 14 logged days, it's surfaced as a gap — with specific foods that are safe for them and dense in what's short. No grades, no alarms, no red screens.

What we won't do

Three promises we designed around

Not marketing lines — constraints written down before building, because each one is a place other apps in this category went wrong.

We will never paywall your own data.

Recording what you fed your child, and getting it back out again, is the reason the app exists — not a premium tier. Barcode scanning and logging stay free.

No AI-invented nutrition. Ever.

Nutrition and allergen information comes from the product's own label data via USDA FoodData Central, with Open Food Facts as a barcode fallback. Nothing is estimated from a photo. A guessed number about a child's allergy is worse than no number at all.

No food moralizing.

No grades, no red-flagging a birthday cupcake, no shame mechanics. Sugars and sodium appear as limits because that's what the guidance says — not as a judgement about your family.

Questions parents ask

Is it really free?

Yes. There's no subscription and no paywall on tracking, planning, scanning or exporting. Some shop links may earn a small commission if you buy through them, which the app discloses where they appear — that's the only commercial element.

Where is my family's data stored?

On your device, in the app's private storage. There is no server holding it. Your NutriNest account exists only on that device — it's there to keep two families' data apart on a shared tablet, not to sign you into anything of ours.

The only things that ever leave are optional food-name searches and barcode numbers sent to public nutrition databases. Never names, profiles, allergies or health information. The privacy policy spells out exactly what and when.

Can I use it for more than one child?

Yes, and it's the reason the app exists. Each child has their own profile, allergies, targets, logs and plans. Serving sizes and nutrient goals are calculated per child.

My child has a serious allergy. Can I rely on this?

Use it as a second pair of eyes, not as verification. NutriNest flags allergens listed in the product's own label data and requires confirmation before logging a match. It cannot know about cross-contact, a manufacturing change, or an unlabelled ingredient. Always read the packaging.

What if a food isn't in the database?

Scan the barcode — the app checks USDA FoodData Central first and falls back to Open Food Facts. If neither has it, enter the values from the label by hand and it's saved for next time.

Is this medical or dietary advice?

No. NutriNest provides general nutrition information based on USDA and CDC dietary reference values. It doesn't diagnose or treat anything. For concerns about your child's growth, eating or health, talk to your pediatrician or a registered dietitian.
