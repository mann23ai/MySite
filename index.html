How we handle data

There is nothing for us to lose

What you record about your children — feeds, sleep, growth, allergies, meals — is written to private storage on your phone and is not sent to us.

This is an architectural choice rather than a promise we ask you to take on faith. Because there's no account on our side and no endpoint to send data to, there is nothing for us to leak, be subpoenaed for, or quietly monetize later. Each app's privacy policy sets out precisely what does and does not leave your device, including the small number of optional lookups the nutrition app makes to public nutrition databases.

No serversNothing of yours is stored off your device.
No analyticsWe can't see how you use the apps. That's the point.
No adsNo advertising SDKs, no ad identifiers.
Delete means deletedUninstall, and it's gone. We hold no copy.

Our story

Why we built this

We're parents of young children. That's the whole origin story, really.

Somewhere between the pediatrician's office and the kitchen table, we started wondering whether our kids were actually getting what they needed — not just enough food, but the right kinds of it. Iron. Calcium. Fiber. Vitamin D. The things that come up once at a check-up and then quietly disappear in the middle of a Tuesday.

So we went looking for something to help us keep track. We tried what was out there. Some of it was built for adults counting calories. Some of it wanted a subscription before it would show us what we'd already typed in ourselves. None of it seemed made for a family with more than one child — each with their own age, their own appetite, their own list of foods they've decided they hate this month.

We couldn't find the thing we wanted. So we built it.

Our kids nutrition app is that one. BabyChart comes from the same instinct, pointed at an earlier stage — when the question is less about nutrients and more about whether anybody has slept.

We're still the ones using both of them every day. That's mostly what keeps them honest.
